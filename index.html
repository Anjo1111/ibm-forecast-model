Our program: return on investment

Program assumptions (paper, Exhibits 2–3): $2bn invested over 2027–28, funded from the M&A budget; revenue ramps to $3bn in 2030 (about one extra point of organic growth a year) at a 30% margin, between Consulting (~12%) and Software (~35%) because the offerings combine implementation with recurring subscriptions. Revenue counts only new offerings and new clients, on top of the base case.

How the model works

Revenue = prior year × (1 + growth), by segment. 2026E growth is fixed for all scenarios (Software +8.5%, Consulting +1%, Infrastructure +2%), in line with 9M actuals and the 4–5% cc guidance. The mainframe-cycle switch adds −3 / +4 / +3 / −4 pp to Infrastructure growth in 2027–30, which nets to zero, so the trend slider stays the average.

Profit = segment revenue × segment margin, with margins moving in a straight line from 2026E to the 2030 target. Corporate & other costs are held at 5.3% of revenue, the level implied by FY25's 18.8% non-GAAP pre-tax margin. Shares stay flat at 953m diluted.

FCF = revenue × FCF margin (2026E: $15.8bn, i.e. FY25 + >$1bn per guidance). Dividends grow 1% a year from $6.3bn. Value per share discounts 2027–30 FCF plus a terminal value at the WACC, then subtracts $40bn core net debt ($63.8bn debt − $12.7bn financing debt − $11.1bn cash, Sept 2026).

Program ROI = (after-tax profit 2027–30 − after-tax investment) ÷ after-tax investment. NPV includes a terminal value on the 2030 profit, so it shows the value of a business that keeps running past 2030.

Sources: IBM FY2024 and FY2025 annual reports; IBM Q3 2026 earnings release (8-K, 21 Oct 2026). FY2024 segment figures are rounded. Net income and EPS are model-derived on a non-GAAP basis, not reported figures.
